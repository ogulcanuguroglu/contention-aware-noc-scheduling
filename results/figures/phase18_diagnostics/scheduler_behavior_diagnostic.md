## 2. HEFT Serialization Analysis

### Observed behavior

**Case 1 (out_tree, CCR=5.0):** HEFT places all 7 tasks on P0 sequentially (makespan = 84.45 AU).  
**Case 2 (fork, CCR=5.0):** HEFT places most tasks on P0, but T3 (vol=12.19) and T7 (vol=30.71) go to P1, confirming that low-volume leaves can escape to other processors when P0 is busy.

### Root cause: HEFT with alpha=0.0, beta=1.0 at CCR=5.0

Communication duration is `duration = beta × vol = vol` (alpha=0 eliminates the hop penalty entirely). At CCR=5.0, typical communication volumes are 60–112 time units. The first task T0 is placed on P0. When HEFT evaluates placing a child task on P1:

```
DRT(P0) = T0.finish  (no comm, T0 is already on P0)
DRT(P1) = T0.finish + vol  (e.g., T0→T1: vol=78.00 → DRT = 14.554 + 78.00 = 92.553)
```

P0's EFT is at most the current tail of P0's schedule + comp_cost. As long as P0's queue delay is less than the incoming communication volume, P0 wins. For large volumes (≥ 60 AU), P0 is dominant for the entire scheduling sequence, causing serialization.

This is **correct HEFT behavior**, not a bug.

### Per-task EFT table (Case 1)

| Task | P0 EFT | P1 EFT | Selected | Reason |
|------|--------|--------|----------|--------|
| T0   | 14.55  | 14.55  | P0       | tie → lowest proc_id wins |
| T1   | 23.60  | 101.60 | P0       | DRT(P1) = 14.55 + 78.00 |
| T2   | 29.22  | 80.92  | P0       | DRT(P1) = 14.55 + 60.75 |
| T5   | 47.91  | 58.47  | P0       | DRT(P1) = 29.22 + 10.56 (small vol); P0 ahead on queue |
| T4   | 65.11  | 126.81 | P0       | DRT(P1) = 14.55 + 103.71 |
| T6   | 79.21  | 133.18 | P0       | DRT(P1) = 14.55 + 89.87 (queue wait absorbed) |
| T3   | 84.45  | 125.92 | P0       | DRT(P1) = 14.55 + 97.07 (wrong estimate; see note) |

Note: T3's actual DRT on P1 uses T1.finish (23.601) + vol(T1,T3) = 23.601 + 97.07 = 120.67, giving EFT = 125.92, well above P0's 84.45.

### Serialization is CCR-driven, not a bug

The same HEFT with zero communication cost immediately parallelizes across 4 processors (see Section 3). The serialization at CCR=5.0 is an accurate response to the dominant communication overhead that exceeds any processor queue delay HEFT can encounter on a DAG with only 7–9 tasks.

## 3. Low-Communication Sanity Tests

### Purpose

To distinguish between "HEFT always serializes" (implementation bug) and "HEFT serializes only when communication cost exceeds queue delay" (correct behavior).

### Results

Three variants were tested for both Case 1 (out_tree) and Case 2 (fork):

**Variant A — communication_volume = 0.0 for all edges:**

| Case | Unique procs used | Makespan |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| out_tree | P0, P1, P2, P3 | 40.800 |
| fork | P0, P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7 | 33.246 |

All independent children spread immediately to separate processors.

**Variant B — CCR = 0.1 (same structure, same seed):**

| Case | Unique procs used | Makespan |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| out_tree | P0, P1, P2, P3 | 40.800 |
| fork | P0, P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7 | 33.828 |

Full parallelism restored at low CCR.

**Variant C — alpha=0.0, beta=0.0 (zero comm cost):**

Identical to Variant A. Full parallelism.

### Interpretation

**HEFT is not buggy.** When communication is free or cheap (Variants A–C), HEFT correctly distributes independent tasks across processors to minimize makespan. The serial schedule at CCR=5.0 is the correct greedy response to the dominant communication volumes that make remote execution more expensive than queue waiting. This behavior is consistent with the established HEFT literature: at high CCR, list schedulers without duplication tend toward serialization because the communication cost to ship data to a free remote processor exceeds the cost of waiting for the local processor to become available.

## 4. CD-LS vs Proposed CA-D on Fork

### Observed behavior

For Case 2 (fork, n_branches=8, CCR=5.0), CD-LS and Proposed CA-D produce **identical task assignments**:

- T0 duplicated onto P0–P7 (7 duplicate instances, TIR = 1.778)
- Each leaf task T1–T8 placed on a separate processor
- makespan = 33.246 AU for both
- communication_count = 0 for both (all communications eliminated by duplication)
- replayed_speedup_vs_heft = 2.778× for both

### Why they are identical on this DAG

The fork DAG has exactly **one ancestor level**: only T0 is a predecessor of any leaf. 

- **CD-LS** evaluates parent-only duplication. For each leaf Tk on candidate processor Pi, it asks: "if I duplicate T0 locally on Pi, does EFT improve?" Since vol(T0→Tk) is large (12–112 AU), the answer is always yes. CD-LS duplicates T0 onto Pi.
- **Proposed CA-D** evaluates greedy recursive ancestor duplication. For each leaf Tk on Pi, it asks the same question under the contention-aware model. CA-D duplicates T0 onto Pi. It then recursively evaluates T0's predecessors — but T0 has **no predecessors** (it is the root). Recursion terminates immediately.

Both schedulers make the identical optimal decision: co-locate T0 with every leaf.

### Contention model difference is invisible here

With alpha=0.0 and no other communications in flight at the moment each leaf is being scheduled, the contention-aware and classic analytic models agree on the timing. No contention occurs because duplication eliminates all inter-processor transfers. The communication_count is 0 in both native schedules and 0 after replay.

### Conclusion

This case illustrates **"duplication helps"** (2.778× replayed speedup) but **not "CA-D differs from CD-LS"**. A DAG with at least two ancestor levels (e.g., the out_tree or fork_join cases) is required to demonstrate the difference between parent-only and recursive ancestor duplication.

**Recommendation for figures:** Keep fig2b and fig2c as the "duplication benefit" story. Label them explicitly as such. Add a note in the caption stating that on a single-level fork DAG, CD-LS and CA-D produce identical schedules; a deeper topology is needed to observe divergence.

## 5. Proposed CA-D Duplicate Audit on Fork-Join

### CA-D schedule for Case 3 (fork_join, n_branches=4, bl=3, CCR=5.0)

```
T0  P0 [0.000, 14.554]  PRIMARY   (root)
T0  P1 [0.000, 14.554]  DUP
T0  P2 [0.000, 14.554]  DUP
T0  P3 [0.000, 14.554]  DUP
T1  P3 [14.554, 23.601] PRIMARY   (branch 0)
T2  P3 [23.601, 29.216] PRIMARY
T3  P3 [29.216, 34.464] PRIMARY
T4  P2 [14.554, 31.753] PRIMARY   (branch 1)
T5  P2 [31.753, 50.445] PRIMARY
T5  P0 [54.694, 73.386] DUP
T6  P2 [50.445, 64.544] PRIMARY
T6  P0 [73.386, 87.485] DUP
T7  P1 [14.554, 30.497] PRIMARY   (branch 2)
T8  P1 [30.497, 43.651] PRIMARY
T9  P1 [43.651, 62.677] PRIMARY
T9  P0 [93.174, 112.200] DUP
T10 P0 [14.554, 31.792] PRIMARY   (branch 3)
T11 P0 [31.792, 36.833] PRIMARY
T12 P0 [36.833, 54.694] PRIMARY
T13 P0 [112.200, 117.704] PRIMARY (sink)
```

### Duplicate-by-duplicate analysis

**T0 on P1, P2, P3 (3 duplicates):** Each enables a branch start task locally:
- T0 dup on P1 → T7 starts at P1 at t=14.554 (local gap = 0)
- T0 dup on P2 → T4 starts at P2 at t=14.554 (local gap = 0)
- T0 dup on P3 → T1 starts at P3 at t=14.554 (local gap = 0)

Remote alternative: vol(T0→T7)=81.95 → arrival = 14.554+81.95 = 96.50, far too late.  
**All three T0 duplicates are necessary.**

**T5 dup on P0 [54.694, 73.386]:** Enables T6 dup on P0.  
Remote alternative for T6 dup from T5 primary (P2): vol(T5,T6)=65.76 → arrival = 50.445+65.76 = 116.205.  
T6 dup needs T5 data by t=73.386. Remote would arrive at 116.205.  
**T5 dup is necessary.**

**T6 dup on P0 [73.386, 87.485]:** Enables T13 primary on P0. T13 starts at 112.200, well after T6 dup finishes (gap = 24.7 AU). T6 dup is not the binding constraint for T13.  
However: could T13 receive T6's data from T6 primary (P2) instead?  
vol(T6,T13)=63.79 → remote arrival = 64.544+63.79 = 128.334 > 112.200.  
Without T6 dup, T13 could not start at 112.200; it would be delayed to at least 128.334.  
**T6 dup is necessary.** The 24.7 AU gap at the end reflects that T9 dup (not T6) is the binding constraint for T13.

**T9 dup on P0 [93.174, 112.200]:** Enables T13 primary on P0 (gap = 0 — this is the binding constraint).  
Remote alternative from T9 primary (P1): vol(T9,T13)=93.23 → arrival = 62.677+93.23 = 155.907.  
Without T9 dup, T13 would start at max(46.19, 128.33, 155.91, 54.69) = 155.91.  
**T9 dup is necessary; it is the binding constraint for T13.**

### Summary: no spurious duplicates

All 6 CA-D duplicates serve as local data sources for at least one successor (usage class A). Phase 15B conservative pruning correctly retained all of them: each duplicate is provably used by a successor on the same processor, and the remote alternative path would arrive too late.

### Why does T13 start at 112.200 AU?

T13 is on P0 and depends on T3 (P3), T6 dup (P0), T9 dup (P0), and T12 (P0):
- T3 on P3: vol(T3,T13)=11.73 → arrival = 34.464+11.73 = 46.19 (remote comm reserved)
- T6 dup on P0: finishes 87.485 (local) ✓
- T9 dup on P0: finishes 112.200 (local) ← binding
- T12 on P0: finishes 54.694 (local) ✓

T9 dup takes 112.200-93.174=19.026 AU to execute (it is the full re-execution of T9), and it cannot start until its predecessor T8 (P1) finishes at 43.651 plus T8's data arrives from P1... wait, actually T9 dup's DRT is driven by T8 primary on P1 finishing at 43.651, plus vol(T8,T9)=41.62 comm to P0 (reserved under contention), arriving at ~93.17, matching exactly.

### CD-LS duplicate T9 behavior (contrast)

CD-LS places T9 dup on P2 (not P0), with T13 primary on P2 at t=118.755. The native model is optimistic: CD-LS does not model link contention, so it underestimates the remote communication delays. Under replay, CD-LS's T9→T13 path and T12→T13 path experience contention, pushing T13 to t≈154.8. That is the CD-LS collapse story (1.246× replay overhead).

## 7. Task Instance Ratio Explanation

**Definition:**

```
TIR = total_task_instance_count / primary_task_count
    = (primary_count + duplicate_count) / primary_count
```

TIR = 1.0 means no duplication. TIR = 2.0 means on average each task has one primary and one duplicate instance. TIR is always ≥ 1.0.

**Examples from Phase 18 figures:**

| Case | Scheduler | Primary | Duplicates | Total | TIR |
|------|-----------|---------|------------|-------|-----|
| Case 1 out_tree | HEFT | 7 | 0 | 7 | 1.000 |
| Case 1 out_tree | CD-LS | 7 | 1 | 8 | 1.143 |
| Case 1 out_tree | Proposed CA-D | 7 | 5 | 12 | 1.714 |
| Case 2 fork | HEFT | 9 | 0 | 9 | 1.000 |
| Case 2 fork | CD-LS | 9 | 7 | 16 | 1.778 |
| Case 2 fork | Proposed CA-D | 9 | 7 | 16 | 1.778 |
| Case 3 fork_join | HEFT | 14 | 0 | 14 | 1.000 |
| Case 3 fork_join | CD-LS | 14 | 4 | 18 | 1.286 |
| Case 3 fork_join | Proposed CA-D | 14 | 6 | 20 | 1.429 |

**Interpretation:** TIR captures the duplication overhead. Proposed CA-D's higher TIR reflects deeper recursive ancestor duplication. The TIR in the Phase 16 aggregate results (CA-D TIR ≈ 2.26 on random DAGs, 1.65 on graph families) is consistent with the per-case values observed here.

**Note for fig2d:** On the fork DAG, CD-LS and CA-D produce TIR = 1.778 because both schedulers make exactly the same duplication decisions (see Section 4). The TIR bars are equal. This is correct but should be noted in the caption.
